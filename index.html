How it works: the recent race sets today's fitness and the goal time sets the fitness needed on race day. Fitness is planned to improve evenly across the build, reaching goal level by the taper. Each phase trains at the fitness expected at its start, and each checkpoint is the fitness expected at its end.

Heart rate: the run's average heart rate, as a share of heart-rate reserve (max minus resting), is taken as the same share of aerobic capacity (Swain's %HRR ≈ %VO2 reserve). That gives an aerobic fitness estimate from heart rate, limited to within 8% of the race score because one run is a noisy reading. The slider blends it into today's easy and steady paces fully, marathon pace at 75%, half marathon and threshold at 50%, 10K at 25%, and leaves 5K and mile paces to the race alone, since a steady run says little about speed. The heart-rate range under each zone name is what this model expects at that zone's effort; as fitness improves, the same heart rate should come at faster paces. 5K and mile reps are too short for heart rate to catch up, so they have none. Heat, hills, dehydration and cardiac drift on runs past about 40 minutes all raise heart rate at a given pace.

Race paces use Daniels' race-equivalence tables, adjusted by the athlete profile slider. Threshold runs from halfway between 10K and half marathon pace out to half marathon pace. Steady is 8–13% slower than marathon pace and easy is 20–32% slower. The timeline check is a rule of thumb: up to about 0.3% faster per week is realistic for most trained runners, and over 0.5% is aggressive.
